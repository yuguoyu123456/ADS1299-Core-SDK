# Charter — Biomedical Engineering & BCI Student Support Initiative

## 1. Purpose

The Biomedical Engineering & BCI Student Support Initiative exists to expand student access to education, research participation, open technology, and responsible innovation in biomedical engineering and brain-computer interfaces.

Its public-interest purpose is broader than the promotion of any single product. The initiative may use ADS1299-Core-SDK and related hardware as educational tools, but student support should remain scientifically open and should not require endorsement or purchase of a particular product.

## 2. Core activities

Subject to available resources, the initiative may support:

- student research mini-grants,
- hardware and component access,
- PCB fabrication support,
- open-source engineering projects,
- educational resources and workshops,
- reproducibility and validation work,
- mentorship,
- student presentations and dissemination, and
- collaborative projects that advance biomedical engineering / BCI education.

## 3. Values

### Scientific integrity

Claims, results, and selection decisions should be evidence-based and documented.

### Open knowledge

Open-source software, open hardware, reproducible methods, and reusable educational resources are encouraged when appropriate and ethically permissible.

### Fair opportunity

Selection criteria should focus on project quality, feasibility, learning value, need, and potential impact rather than prestige alone.

### Inclusion

The initiative should seek to reduce unnecessary barriers for students from institutions or regions with limited access to specialized biomedical engineering resources.

### Independence

Donors, sponsors, vendors, and project maintainers should not automatically control scientific conclusions or grant-selection outcomes.

### Transparency

Program rules, award criteria, and aggregate outcomes should be publicly documented where privacy and legal requirements permit.

## 4. Beneficiaries

Primary beneficiaries are students and early-stage trainees in fields related to:

- biomedical engineering,
- BCI / neural engineering,
- neuroscience,
- biosignal acquisition,
- embedded systems,
- signal processing,
- rehabilitation engineering,
- medical instrumentation, and
- related interdisciplinary research.

Individual grant calls may define narrower eligibility.

## 5. Ethical boundaries

The initiative does not waive or replace institutional requirements for human-subject research, animal research, clinical studies, data privacy, biosafety, or electrical safety.

Support may be declined or suspended when a project lacks required ethical approval, presents unacceptable safety risk, misrepresents regulatory status, or conflicts with applicable law.

## 6. Organizational status

This initiative is currently a project-level support program and planning framework. It is not represented as a legally registered charitable foundation, nonprofit organization, or tax-exempt entity unless and until such status is formally established.

## 7. International organizations and SDGs

The initiative may state that its activities are aligned with public-interest themes reflected in the UN Sustainable Development Goals. This is thematic alignment only and must not be represented as United Nations endorsement, accreditation, affiliation, or partnership.

## 8. Amendments

Governance documents may evolve as the initiative grows. Material changes should be documented through repository history so that the evolution of the program remains auditable.
