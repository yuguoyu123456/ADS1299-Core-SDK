# Governance Framework

## 1. Governance objective

The purpose of this framework is to make student-support decisions reviewable, consistent, and increasingly independent as the initiative grows.

## 2. Roles

A future grant round should identify, at minimum:

- **Program lead** — responsible for the call, documentation, and final process integrity.
- **Reviewers** — assess applications using published criteria.
- **Finance / resource custodian** — records funds or in-kind resources when applicable.
- **Technical advisers** — provide domain expertise without automatically controlling award decisions.

One person may temporarily perform multiple roles during an early pilot, but this should be disclosed.

## 3. Decision process

Each grant or support call should publish before applications open:

- available support,
- applicant eligibility,
- project eligibility,
- application deadline,
- evaluation criteria,
- review method,
- conflict-of-interest rules,
- expected reporting obligations, and
- decision timeline.

Selection decisions should be recorded with a short rationale and retained for accountability.

## 4. Review principles

Review should consider factors such as:

- scientific / engineering merit,
- feasibility,
- student learning value,
- reproducibility,
- resource need,
- responsible research design,
- potential public benefit, and
- fit with the specific call.

The initiative should avoid using social status, institutional prestige, personal relationships, or product-purchase history as hidden selection criteria.

## 5. Conflict of interest

Reviewers must disclose relevant personal, academic, supervisory, financial, commercial, or organizational conflicts before reviewing an application.

Where a material conflict exists, the reviewer should normally recuse from scoring or decision-making for that application.

See `CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md`.

## 6. Financial / in-kind transparency

For each funded round, the initiative should record:

- total resources available,
- source of resources,
- number of applications,
- number of awards,
- total amount or value awarded,
- award categories, and
- unused or carried-forward resources.

Where public disclosure of individual amounts would create privacy or legal concerns, aggregate reporting may be used.

## 7. Separation from product sales

Grant eligibility should not require applicants to buy ADS1299 hardware or other products from project maintainers.

When project hardware is provided in-kind, its approximate value and the fact that it is an in-kind award should be disclosed.

## 8. Complaints and review

Future public grant calls should provide a method for applicants to report procedural concerns, conflicts of interest, discrimination, or misuse of resources.

The process may distinguish between:

- requests for clarification,
- procedural complaints, and
- appeals, where appeals are offered.

## 9. Privacy

Applicant personal data should be collected only when necessary for program administration. Public reports should avoid unnecessary disclosure of personal information.

## 10. Evolution

As funding volume and organizational complexity increase, the initiative should consider additional safeguards such as independent reviewers, multi-person approval, external financial oversight, or formal nonprofit governance.
