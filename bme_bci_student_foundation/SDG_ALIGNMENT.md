# Sustainable Development Goal Alignment

This document explains how the Biomedical Engineering & BCI Student Support Initiative may align with selected United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

**Important:** SDG alignment is a thematic mapping only. It does not imply United Nations endorsement, accreditation, partnership, funding, membership, or affiliation.

## SDG 3 — Good Health and Well-being

Potential alignment includes:

- student research in biomedical engineering and physiological signal acquisition,
- development of research tools for neuroscience and brain-computer interfaces,
- training related to responsible biomedical technology development, and
- research-capacity building that may contribute to health-related innovation.

Official reference: https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal3

## SDG 4 — Quality Education

This is one of the initiative's strongest areas of alignment.

Potential contributions include:

- improving access to technical and scientific learning resources,
- supporting tertiary-level engineering and research participation,
- practical training in electronics, embedded systems, signal processing, neuroscience, and BCI,
- open educational resources, and
- small-scale student support designed to reduce barriers to advanced technical training.

Official reference: https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal4

## SDG 9 — Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

Potential alignment includes:

- open-source biomedical hardware and software,
- reproducible engineering,
- low-cost research infrastructure,
- student participation in research and development, and
- technology-capacity building.

Official reference: https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal9

## SDG 10 — Reduced Inequalities

Potential alignment may exist when programs intentionally improve access for students who face geographic, institutional, financial, disability-related, or other barriers to research participation.

Any such claim should be supported by actual program design and outcome data rather than general statements alone.

Official reference: https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal10

## SDG 17 — Partnerships for the Goals

Potential alignment includes transparent collaboration among students, universities, laboratories, open-source communities, nonprofit organizations, industry contributors, and other research-support partners.

A collaboration should only be called a formal partnership when the participating organizations have actually established that relationship.

Official reference: https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal17

## How to describe alignment publicly

Recommended wording:

> The initiative's activities are designed to contribute to areas represented in the UN Sustainable Development Goals, particularly health, quality education, innovation, inclusion, and research partnerships. This statement describes thematic alignment and does not imply endorsement or affiliation by the United Nations.

Avoid wording such as:

- "UN-certified project"
- "United Nations foundation"
- "official UN BCI program"
- "UN-approved ADS1299 hardware"
- "United Nations partner"

unless that specific status has been formally granted and can be independently verified.

## Evidence-based impact reporting

Future SDG-related reporting should use measurable project indicators where possible, for example:

- number of students supported,
- countries / regions represented,
- number and value of grants or hardware awards,
- open educational resources published,
- research prototypes completed,
- reproducible datasets / code / designs released,
- student presentations or publications,
- mentorship hours, and
- follow-up outcomes.

The initiative should distinguish clearly between **activities**, **outputs**, **outcomes**, and long-term **impact**.
